CETI visuals — v1

The Phonetics of a Sperm Whale,
read from its codas.

Four short visual reproductions of Sharma et al. (2024). Each one tries to make a single claim of the paper legible from the data alone: that what sounds like a single click pattern is in fact a four-axis system — rhythm, tempo, rubato, ornamentation — and that those axes combine into a much larger vocabulary than was first catalogued.

Sharma, P. et al. Contextual and Combinatorial Structure in Sperm Whale Vocalisations. Nat. Commun. (2024). Data: Project CETI EC-1, 2005–2018.

Section 01 — headlineThe exchange

An exchange is two whales overlapping each other for a few minutes. Each coda is a small staircase of clicks; the vertical axis is wall-clock time, the horizontal axis is the click index inside the coda. When you line up consecutive codas of the same speaker, two things become visible: rubato — the gentle tempo drift between adjacent codas — and ornamentation — a single extra click tacked onto the end. Both are conversational, not stylistic.

One coda = one staircase. Caller A in paper blue, Caller B in paper orange. A circle ◯ marks an ornament. Hover any click for its ICI in milliseconds.

Section 02 — combinatoricsA phonetic alphabet

Project every coda onto its rhythm cluster (18 of them) and its tempo cluster (5 of them). The resulting 18 × 5 grid is the paper's headline arithmetic — the larger inventory the four axes permit. Each cell carries its raw coda count, its ornament rate, and the mean magnitude of rubato drift inside it. Hover a cell to highlight the exchanges that contain its codas in the section above.

Cell shading uses log(count + 1). Inset pies show orn % (amber) and |Δ rubato| proxy (teal, longer arc = more tempo modulation in that cell).

Section 03 — context dependencyConditioning on context

Rhythm and tempo are largely context-independent. Rubato and ornament are not — they depend on what the partner just said. The left panel shows the distribution of rubato drift, the duration delta between two successive same-speaker codas. The right panel shows the ratio of last-ICI to median-preceding-ICI, separating plain from ornamented codas. The toggle restricts to same-tempo-cluster pairs.

Rubato drift centred on zero; ornament codas have a distinctly longer final ICI. Both are downstream of the conversational partner.

Section 04 — repertoireThe repertoire, projected

A linear projection of every coda into two dimensions, using only its rhythm one-hot, its log-duration, and its ornament flag. PCA is the honest minimum here; the goal is not faithful manifold structure but a visible separation of the 18-rhythm families. Colour by tempo cluster.

Each point is a coda. PCA-2 on [rhythm one-hot ⊕ log-duration ⊕ ornament].

CETI visuals — v2

The Whale Acoustics Model,
read from its tokens.

Three counterparts to the WhAM paper. Where v1 reads codas as discrete categories, WhAM reads them as audio token sequences and trains a transformer to predict, translate, and generate them. The visuals here operationalise the three moves WhAM makes: generation from latent control, translation from foreign acoustic prompts, and iterative unmasking of partially-revealed sequences. None of this runs a real transformer in the browser — the goal is to make each move legible from a small surrogate.

Paradise, O. et al. WhAM: Towards A Translative Model of Sperm Whale Vocalization. NeurIPS 2025 (arXiv:2512.02206). Visuals are conceptual, not model outputs.

Section 01 — generationPseudocoda playground

WhAM samples from a learned acoustic distribution. Here we expose the four axes from Sharma et al. as direct controls and compose the ICI sequence by hand: pick a rhythm shape (normalised cumulative ICI centroid), scale by tempo, drift each repeat by a fixed rubato, optionally append an ornament click. This is the feature-level analogue of WhAM's generation pipeline — what changes when you turn each knob.

Each staircase is one synthetic coda. Across repeats, the tempo drifts by ±rubato seconds per repeat. The ornament toggle duplicates the final ICI as a trailing click. Audio = 1 ms exponentially decaying sine bursts at 5 kHz, played at the ICI timestamps.

Section 02 — translationTranslation lattice

WhAM translates non-coda audio into coda acoustic style; the Fréchet Audio Distance to natural codas drops dramatically. We can't run the model in the browser, but we can pose the geometric question: given a few synthetic input prompts in feature space, where does each one land when projected onto the nearest natural-coda neighbourhood? The dotted baseline is the median pairwise distance among natural codas — the threshold below which WhAM's output is FAD-indistinguishable.

Background grey = natural-coda PCA cloud (same projection as v1 §4). Foreground markers = synthetic input prompts. With translate off, each prompt sits in its own corner of feature space. On, each animates to its nearest natural-coda neighbour; the diamond is the post-translation position, the line is the path.

Section 03 — iterative unmaskToken-stream unmask

VampNet (WhAM's base) tokenises audio, masks a random subset of tokens, and generates the missing ones via iterative parallel decoding — each step fills the most-confident positions first. Here we run the same loop at the ICI level: take a real coda, mask 40 % of its positions, and fill them one round at a time using the natural-coda cloud as a nearest-neighbour oracle. The certainty heat-strip below the tiles shows how confident the predictor is at each remaining masked position.

One tile per click position; colour bins the ICI (faster = blue, slower = amber). Grey tiles are masked. Each step reveals the most-confident masked positions in parallel; below, the certainty strip (1 − normalised distance) shrinks as the predictor commits.

CETI visuals — v3

The hydroacoustic frame,
read across the deployment.

Three canonical hydroacoustic displays, applied to the EC-1 coda corpus. A single playback clock sweeps the 2005–2018 record across all three views at once: a click-train rasterplot makes each exchange feel alive, an LTSA-style heat-clock collapses 8,719 codas into a 13-year density grid, and a stylised PPI sweep tracks which rhythm clusters dominate as the deployment unfolds. Pausable; speed-controllable; audio is opt-in.

Display vocabulary per HYDROACOUSTICS.md. Source data: Project CETI EC-1 corpus (Sharma et al., 2024). No PII; no framework; vanilla ES modules with canvas 2D and SVG.

2005-01

Section 01 — exchange-scaleClick-train rasterplot

One coda = one horizontal lane. Each click on that coda is a vertical tick positioned by its cumulative ICI. The teal playhead sweeps left-to-right at the global tempo; as it crosses each tick, the tick flashes and decays over about 300 ms. The result is a tactile view of how exchanges unfold in real time — speakers overlapping, ornaments tagging the tail of a coda, rubato visible as tempo drift across consecutive same-colour lanes.

Caller A in paper blue, Caller B in paper orange. Amber rings flag ornamented coda tails. The audio toggle synthesises a 5 kHz exp-decay click per tick — opt-in, scheduled from the same cursor as the playhead.

Section 02 — corpus-scaleLTSA corpus heat-clock

Long-Term Spectral Average is the canonical hydroacoustic move for collapsing a long deployment into a single readable frame. Dataset 1 of sw-combinatoriality carries the calendar Date for each of its 8,719 codas but no time-of-day, so the LTSA here reduces to a 2-D density: months across (2005–2017, 156 columns), day-of-week down (Mon–Sun). The teal cursor sweeps left-to-right at the global tempo; click any cell to jump.

Cell shading = log(count + 1), baked through an OKLCH magma-like ramp (perceptually uniform — no rainbow). Dataset 1 carries date but not time-of-day; LTSA reduces to deployment-month density.

Section 03 — categoricalPPI sonar sweep

Plan Position Indicator: a rotating radar-style sweep. Eighteen spokes, one per rhythm cluster, distributed uniformly around the bearing circle. Spoke length = number of codas in that rhythm cluster within a sliding window centred on the global clock cursor. The sweep rotates at six rpm (one full revolution per ten seconds at 1×), leaving a teal alpha-decay trail.

Stylised PPI. Rhythm clusters are mapped to bearing for visualisation — not a real bearing-array output. The sweep timing is the deployment timeline scrubbing through the dataset.
